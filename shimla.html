About Shimla

Shimla, the state capital, served as the summer capital of British India from 1864. The district blends Victorian architecture — the Viceregal Lodge, Christ Church, Gaiety Theatre — with dense cedar and rhododendron forests. Beyond the city, Shimla district offers Narkanda for skiing, Rampur for tribal culture, Chail for the world's highest cricket ground, and Rohru for off-the-beaten-path apple orchards.

Top Places to Visit (10)

  • The Ridge & Mall RoadThe Ridge is a broad open promenade at 2,213 m that formed the ceremonial and social heart of British Shimla — the summer capital of India from 1864 to 1939. Christ Church (1857), one of the oldest churches in northern India, anchors the eastern end with its distinctive neo-Gothic facade and stained glass windows. The Mall Road, descending west from the Ridge, retains many of its original Victorian commercial buildings including the Gaiety Theatre (1887), which still hosts live performances. The traffic-free promenade offers uninterrupted panoramas of the Shivalik and Dhauladhar ranges on clear winter mornings when the air is sharp and the distant peaks carry fresh snow.

  • Jakhu TempleRising 300 m above the Ridge to 2,455 m, Jakhu Hill is the highest point of the Shimla ridge and home to one of Himachal's most visited Hanuman temples. The 2 km forest trail from the Ridge passes through a cedar reserve inhabited by a large and bold population of rhesus monkeys — keep belongings securely stowed. A ropeway (gondola) now links the Ridge to the hilltop for those who prefer not to trek. The giant 33-metre Hanuman statue, visible from much of central Shimla, was installed in 2010 and has become a landmark in its own right. Dawn visits on clear days reward walkers with views extending to the plains of Punjab.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Viceregal LodgeCompleted in 1888 as the official summer residence of the British Viceroy of India, this imposing Scottish baronial mansion set on Observatory Hill is now the Indian Institute of Advanced Study. The grey limestone building, surrounded by terraced gardens, saw some of the most consequential meetings of the colonial era — the Shimla Conference of 1945, which shaped the Partition of India, was held here. Guided tours (ticket required) take visitors through the state rooms, library, and the original Viceregal office where key decisions that shaped the subcontinent were made. The gardens and perimeter walkway offer scenic forest views and are open to the public daily.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • KufriSixteen kilometres east of Shimla at 2,622 m, Kufri sits on an exposed ridge that catches heavy snowfall from December to February, making it the most accessible skiing and snow-play destination from the state capital. The Himalayan Nature Park on the ridge holds snow leopards, Himalayan brown bears, and several Himalayan pheasant species in semi-natural enclosures. In summer, the meadows become a base for yak rides and short walks to Mahasu Peak (2,850 m) with 360° views across the Shimla district. The drive from Shimla winds through apple orchards and cedar groves that peak in late September when the fruit hangs heavy on the branches.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • NarkandaPositioned at 2,708 m on the old Hindustan-Tibet Road, Narkanda is Shimla district's highest significant town and a notable apple-growing belts in Asia. The apple orchards cover the surrounding slopes in a sea of white blossom in May, and from late August to October the roadsides are lined with wooden crates of freshly picked fruit heading to the plains. The Government-run ski slopes on the Hatu ridge (open January–March) are among India's better-equipped facilities, with a gentle beginner's area and more challenging descent. Hatu Peak temple at 3,400 m is a 7 km drive from town on a rough road, reached through dense forest that opens to an extraordinary 360° alpine panorama.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • ChailThe Chail hill station, split between Solan and Shimla districts, was created in 1891 by Maharaja Bhupinder Singh of Patiala after the British banned him from Shimla following a dispute. Determined to create a retreat superior to the summer capital, he acquired 5,500 acres of forest on the Shivalik ridge and built the Chail Palace at 2,250 m — now a heritage hotel managed by the Himachal Tourism Corporation. The palace grounds contain the Chail Cricket Ground, built in 1893 at 2,444 m and Guinness-certified as the world's highest playing cricket ground, which still hosts matches. The extensive forest sanctuary surrounding the palace complex contains pheasants, barking deer and leopard.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • RampurStraddling the Satluj river at 1,000 m, Rampur Bushahr was over many generations the most important trading post on the Tibet road — the place where Central Asian caravans met the plains merchants. The annual Lavi Fair (November) has operated here for over 300 years and remains one of Himachal's great commercial and cultural gatherings. The Padam Palace, once the seat of the Bushahr royal family, displays a fascinating blend of Mughal, Tibetan and European architectural influences. The town sits at the gateway to Kinnaur and its bazaar is a good last stop for supplies before entering the narrowing Satluj gorge.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • RohruThe Pabbar river, a Satluj tributary draining the Rohru valley, runs cold and clear through one of Shimla district's most authentic apple-growing landscapes. The town is a small administrative centre at 1,525 m, but the valleys radiating north and west contain orchards of a quality that supply premium export-grade fruit. The Pabbar is a recognised brown trout fishery with a Forest Department permit system. Chirgaon, 30 km north on a winding road, is the base for winter snow walks and connects to the Chanshal Pass route (4,520 m) that opens in June. Almost no international tourists reach Rohru, making it among the most genuinely offbeat valley experiences in the district.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • TattapaniThe name means 'hot water' in Hindi, and Tattapani's natural sulphur springs have attracted visitors to this Satluj riverside site for generations. The springs themselves are now partially submerged behind the Koldam dam reservoir, but several functional bathing facilities remain accessible. River rafting on the Satluj between Tattapani and Sunni — a 14 km stretch through scenic gorges — is the most popular adventure activity from October to April. The temperature here is significantly warmer than Shimla in winter, making it a practical short-break destination from the capital during cold spells when Shimla is icy.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Hatu PeakThe highest point in the Narkanda area at 3,400 m, Hatu Peak is reached by a paved road that winds through dense fir and deodar forest before emerging at an exposed rocky summit where an ancient Hatu Mata temple has stood over many generations. The 360° view from the summit takes in the Kinnaur Kailash range to the east, the Dhauladhar to the northwest, the Shimla ridges to the south and the Spiti peaks beyond Rohtang. Snow leopard pugmarks have been reported in the forest below the peak, and This area falls within a wildlife corridor. Sunrise from the summit, reached by a pre-dawn drive from Narkanda, is one of the district's finest photographic experiences.
