About Chamba

Chamba is one of Himachal's most underrated districts. The old Chamba town has preserved ancient temples dating back 1,000+ years. Dalhousie, a former British cantonment, offers colonial charm and access to Dainkund Peak. Khajjiar is often described as the 'Mini Switzerland of India' — a circular meadow ringed by deodars with a small lake. The Ravi and Chenab rivers drain through this westernmost district.

Top Places to Visit in Chamba (10)

  • DalhousieBuilt in the 1860s as a hill station for British troops who could not afford to summer in Shimla, Dalhousie spreads across five hills at an average altitude of 2,036 m in the Dhauladhar foothills. The cantonment character — churches, bungalows with verandas, pine-lined Mall roads — is better preserved here than in almost any other HP hill station, partly because the town never attracted significant industry or high-rise construction. Subhash Chowk, the main square, anchors the central ridge; the two Mall roads, Upper and Lower, offer forest walks with views toward the Pir Panjal on clear days. The writer Rabindranath Tagore and revolutionary Bhagat Singh both spent formative periods here. Dalhousie is cold and green in summer, snow-covered and scenic in winter.

  • KhajjiarTwenty-two km from Dalhousie through dense deodar forest, Khajjiar occupies a circular natural plateau of approximately 1.5 km diameter at 1,920 m — a bowl of short-cropped grass around a small lake, enclosed by an unbroken ring of mature deodar trees, so symmetrically scenic that it has been officially designated as one of 160 locations worldwide similar to Switzerland, earning the 'Mini Switzerland of India' title from the Swiss Ambassador in 1992. The Khajji Nag temple on the lake shore, dedicated to a serpent deity, is an ancient wooden structure predating the tourism. Horse riding, zorbing and paragliding are offered on the meadow. The sunset light through the deodars onto the lake is especially atmospheric.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Chamba TownThe ancient capital of the Chamba kingdom, continuously settled for over 1,500 years, lies in a bend of the Ravi river at 996 m — unusually low for a Himachal town, giving it a warm, almost subtropical climate compared to the surrounding ranges. The Chaugan, a long open maidan in the town centre, is used for the Minjar Fair (August) — a week-long festival in which gods from 80+ surrounding villages are brought together in procession. The Lakshmi Narayan temple complex on the hillside above the Chaugan contains six stone shikharas from the 10th–19th centuries, the oldest being among the finest examples of the Pratihara architectural style in the western Himalaya. Chamba is also renowned for its Rumal — hand-embroidered silk cloth using a double-sided technique unique to the region.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Dainkund PeakThe highest accessible peak near Dalhousie at 2,755 m, Dainkund (sometimes called 'Singing Hill') is reached by a 2 km trail from the Dalhousie ridge that passes through meadows of wild strawberry and alpine herbs before reaching the rocky summit. The Pohlani Mata temple at the top is tended by a family of priests who live in the adjoining shelter throughout the year, even through heavy winter snowfall. The summit panorama takes in the Pir Panjal range to the north, the Ravi valley 1,000 m below, and on exceptionally clear winter mornings the plains of Punjab extending to the horizon. The Indian Air Force maintains an installation on the ridge below the summit.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Kalatop Wildlife SanctuaryBetween Dalhousie and Khajjiar, the 30 sq km Kalatop Sanctuary protects a notable surviving blocks of Himalayan moist temperate forest in the Chamba district — a dense canopy of deodar, silver fir, oak and rhododendron that receives snowfall for 3–4 months annually. The Kalatop Khajjiar Reserve contains black bear, leopard, yellow-throated marten, barking deer and over 100 bird species including the cheer pheasant (endangered). A forest rest house in the middle of the sanctuary offers basic accommodation bookable through the Forest Department — among the most atmospheric lodging experiences in Chamba. Walking trails through the sanctuary are open; a mandatory forest guard accompanies visits to the interior.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Manimahesh LakeAt 4,080 m below the sacred peak of Manimahesh Kailash (5,653 m), this glacial lake is one of the holiest sites in Himachal Pradesh — Shiva is believed to reside on the mountain above, and the lake is considered his bathing pool. The annual Manimahesh Yatra (August–September, timed to the Radha Ashtami festival) brings up to 100,000 pilgrims along a 13 km trail from Hadsar, making it one of the Himalayas' great devotional journeys. Outside the yatra season the trail is almost empty and the landscape — moraines, alpine tundra, the dark lake and the white pyramid of Manimahesh — is strikingly austere. The approach road from Bharmour to Hadsar passes through the sacred Gaddi tribal homeland.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Lakshmi Narayan TempleThe six-temple complex of Lakshmi Narayan in Chamba town represents the finest concentration of early medieval stone temple architecture in Himachal Pradesh. The oldest and largest temple, dedicated to Lakshmi Narayan (Vishnu), was built in the Shikhara style in the 10th century by Raja Sahil Varman, who is said to have diverted the Saho spring to bring water to the town. The stone carvings on the exterior walls — Vishnu in his ten avatars, Shiva with Parvati, celestial musicians and guardian figures — are of museum quality. Inside the temples, bronze castings and silver-repoussé panels from the 10th–17th centuries are displayed. The temples are active sites of worship and the priests are happy to explain the iconography.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • BharmaurAt 2,130 m on the Budhil river, Bharmaur was the original capital of the Chamba kingdom for 400 years before Raja Sahil Varman moved the court to Chamba town in the 10th century. The Chaurasi (84) temple complex at its centre — a courtyard of 84 ancient shrines from the 7th to 10th centuries — ranks among the most significant ancient religious sites in the western Himalaya and yet receives only a trickle of visitors because of the difficult access. The main Manimahesh and Lakshmi Narayan temples in the Chaurasi complex are still active. Bharmaur is also the cultural and economic centre of the Gaddi tribe — the pastoral transhumant community whose shepherds take their flocks over 4,000 m passes between the Chamba winter pastures and the Lahaul summer meadows.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Sach PassAt 4,390 m, the Sach Pass ranks among the most technically demanding high-altitude roads in India — a single-lane track carved into sheer rock faces above a 2,000 m void, crossing the Great Himalayan Range to link the Chamba Valley with the Pangi Valley. The road is unpaved, subject to landslides and snowfall from October to June, and passable only in 4WD vehicles with experienced drivers. The pass is flanked by permanent snowfields even in August, and the final approach involves river crossings and switchbacks of extreme gradient. This place is precisely this difficulty that makes the journey extraordinary: the view from the pass north into the Pangi valley is of a landscape that looks quite different from the better-known in the accessible parts of Himachal.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Pangi ValleyThe Pangi Valley of the upper Chenab river is among the most isolated inhabited valleys in India — cut off by snow for five to six months annually, with road access only through the Sach Pass or the long route through Lahaul. The Pangwali people who inhabit Pangi's 40+ villages have a distinct culture, dialect and traditional costume that has been preserved by centuries of seasonal isolation. The valley receives almost no tourists outside the July–September window, and those who arrive find landscape of extraordinary severity: narrow gorges, rushing rapids and steep slopes with tiny terraced fields clinging to impossible angles. The Forest Department rest house at Kilar village is the only formal accommodation.
