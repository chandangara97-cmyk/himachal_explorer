About Hamirpur

Hamirpur is Himachal's smallest district by area and has the highest literacy rate in the state (and historically among the highest for any district in India). This place is the political heartland of Himachal — home to several chief ministers. The district is noted for Sujanpur Tira, a princely town with a historic fort and riverside ghats on the Beas. It offers an authentic slice of Himachali culture without tourist crowds.

Top Places to Visit in Hamirpur (10)

  • Sujanpur TiraFounded in 1748 by Raja Abhay Chand of the Kangra kingdom on a horseshoe bluff above the Beas river, Sujanpur Tira is a notable examples of an 18th-century planned Himalayan town — a grid of lanes centred on the fort complex with eight major temple complexes arranged around a large maidan. The town reached its cultural peak under the patronage of Raja Sansar Chand (1775–1823), who turned Sujanpur into a centre of Kangra miniature painting, music and drama. The Holi fair at Sujanpur (March) is the most celebrated festival in Hamirpur district, drawing participants for a week of music and colours on the riverside maidan. The ghats on the Beas below the fort are scenic at dawn.

  • Tarapur FortThe fort at Sujanpur Tira, built by Raja Abhay Chand in 1748 and expanded by Raja Sansar Chand in the late 18th century, is formally often called Tarapur Fort — a reference to the local name for the bluff above the Beas. The fort's inner courts, now partially restored, display fragments of the Kangra-school wall paintings that once covered extensive interiors during Sansar Chand's reign. The fort commands an extensive view of the Beas valley, the Dhauladhar range to the north, and the Hamirpur–Kangra ridge to the south. The adjacent Narbadeshwar temple complex, with its multiple shrines from the 18th century, is active and scenicly maintained by local priests.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Deotsidh TempleOn a forested ridge above the Beas river 12 km from Hamirpur town, the Deotsidh temple of Baba Balak Nath ranks among the most visited pilgrimage sites in the Punjab-Himachal belt — receiving an estimated 5–7 million visitors annually, predominantly from Punjab, Haryana and Delhi. Balak Nath is a child saint of the Nath tradition, believed to have meditated in the cave that now forms the inner sanctum of the temple. The cave shrine, where a naturally formed stalagmite is venerated as the saint's image, can be reached to devotees after a short climb on stone steps. The surrounding hillside now includes with gardens, dhaba facilities and pilgrim shelters. The Mela (fair) held on Sundays and on Navratri is particularly large.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Hamirpur TownThe district headquarters at 786 m is a compact, well-planned administrative town that reflects Hamirpur's status as the most literate district in Himachal Pradesh (literacy rate consistently above 88%). The National Institute of Technology Hamirpur, one of India's premier technical institutions, is headquartered here and gives the town a pronounced young and academic character. The town has little traditional heritage tourism value but serves as an efficient base for exploring Sujanpur Tira (18 km) and Deotsidh (12 km). The Hamirpur HRTC bus stand is one of the best-connected in the district, with direct services to Shimla, Manali, Dharamshala and Delhi.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • NadaunOn the banks of the Beas river 22 km east of Hamirpur, Nadaun is historically significant as the site of the Battle of Nadaun (1691) in which Guru Gobind Singh, allied with the Kangra hill rajas, defeated the Mughal forces of Alif Khan — an engagement documented in the Guru's autobiography, the Bachitar Natak. The Beas at Nadaun is wide and calm, with sand and gravel beaches that attract local visitors for bathing. The old ghats and the Nadaun fort ruins are evocative if fragmentary heritage sites. The town sits on the old caravan route from the Punjab plains to the Kullu Valley, and a weekly market (Monday) retains something of the traditional trading fair character.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Awah DeviThe Awah Devi temple, set on a forested ridge with commanding views over both the Hamirpur and Kangra valleys, is an important local pilgrimage centre for the communities of southern Hamirpur district. The goddess is associated with the protection of cattle and the welfare of farming families — a distinctly agricultural deity whose festivals are timed to the agricultural calendar. The forest ridge on which the temple sits is dense with oak and has good populations of langur monkeys. The viewpoint adjacent to the main temple shrine offers a panorama that on clear winter days extends from the Dhauladhar range northward to the Kullu valley peaks.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • BhotaA river village on the left bank of the Beas between Hamirpur and the Dehra Gopipur bridge, Bhota is the site of a traditional market (haat) that has operated on fixed days for generations, drawing farmers from surrounding villages with seasonal produce. The Beas here flows through a wide gravel bed with good fishing for mahseer (the large carp of Himalayan rivers) in the pre-monsoon and post-monsoon windows. The riverside landscape with its poplar windbreaks, mustard fields and distant Dhauladhar backdrop is the quintessential lower Beas valley scene. Basic accommodation is available in the village for those wishing to base themselves here for river activities.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • BijhariA traditional Himachali village 8 km from Sujanpur Tira, Bijhari preserves examples of the vernacular domestic architecture of the lower Beas valley — two-storey stone-and-timber houses with carved woodwork, slate roofs and enclosed courtyards that are increasingly rare as concrete construction replaces traditional building. Homestay initiatives in several families here offer an immersive experience of Himachali agricultural and domestic life: participating in wheat harvest, learning to prepare local foods including siddu and madra, and accompanying the family's cattle to the forest pastures. The views toward the Dhauladhar from the village edge are excellent in clear winter weather.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • BanganaThe market town of Bangana on the Hamirpur-Una border is primarily a commercial hub serving the lowland agricultural belt of southern Hamirpur and northern Una. The town's weekly market (Thursday) is a significant local economic event. The surrounding lowland landscape — mixed cultivation of maize, wheat, sugarcane and vegetables in the warmer zone below 600 m — is representative of the subtropical fringe of Himachal where the hill ecology transitions to the Punjab plains. You will find no significant heritage sites in Bangana itself, but it serves as a functional waypoint for travellers moving between the Hamirpur hills and the Una flatlands.
    Open in Google Maps →
  • Ranser LakeA small reservoir on the edge of Hamirpur town created by damming the Ranser stream, this modest water body now includes as a recreational lake and picnic ground. Winter brings migratory waterfowl including bar-headed geese, pochards and various duck species to the lake — making it a surprisingly rewarding birdwatching site for a peri-urban location. The lake shore is planted with ornamental trees and has walking paths, a boating facility and seasonal food stalls. This place is primarily a local amenity but offers a pleasant hour of outdoor relaxation in combination with a visit to Hamirpur town.
